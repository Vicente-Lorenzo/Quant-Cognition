\section{Survey Research: Identifying the State-of-the-Art}

The review began with surveys, which give the shape of a field faster than individual papers do and point back to the work that established each result.

Hambly et al. \cite{hambly_recent_2023} provided the theoretical basis. The survey sets out reinforcement learning and neural networks separately, then covers how the two combine into deep reinforcement learning and how the result has been applied in finance. It gives the formulations and pseudocode for critic-only, actor-only and actor-critic algorithms together with the papers that introduced each of them, and it is the reference used while writing \Cref{Chapter:Background}.

Fischer \cite{fischer_reinforcement_2018} served the opposite purpose. It is concerned with practice rather than theory, and it reviews implementations rather than derivations. Its value here was the set of dimensions along which it compares studies: the dataset, the network beneath the reinforcement learning agent, the state space, the action space, the reward function, the techniques used against the bias-variance and exploration-exploitation trade-offs, and the training procedure. Those dimensions were adopted as the columns of \Cref{Tables:BasicRelatedWork} and \Cref{Tables:AdvancedRelatedWork}, so that the articles reviewed below could be compared consistently rather than described one at a time.

Three further surveys were consulted \cite{pricope_deep_2021, alameer_reinforcement_2022, meng_reinforcement_2019}. They agree on the broad picture: the field is dominated by equity markets, daily data and discrete trading decisions, and reported results are hard to compare because the trading environment differs from paper to paper.
