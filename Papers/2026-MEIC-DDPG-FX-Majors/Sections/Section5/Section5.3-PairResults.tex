\subsection{Results Across the Seven Majors}

Table~\ref{Tables:PairResults} gives the result for each pair, in alphabetical order. Total return over the eleven years ranges from $+226.6\%$ on USD/CAD, the strongest, to $+15.7\%$ on USD/JPY, the weakest. Every pair is profitable, and every pair is profitable from all five starting balances.

Figure~\ref{Figure:PairEquity} shows each agent against buying and holding its own pair. The shapes differ more than the totals suggest. USD/CAD and GBP/USD grow in steps, with long flat periods between them. EUR/USD is nearly flat for the first six years and produces most of its return after 2021. USD/CHF rises slowly and almost without interruption.

USD/CHF deserves separate attention, because it is the best model in the set on every risk measure and the third worst on return. It returns $+50.6\%$ with a Sharpe ratio of $0.802$, a Sortino ratio of $1.552$, a Calmar ratio of $0.492$ and a maximum drawdown of $8.2\%$, and it does so in $220$ trades over eleven years, roughly one position every three weeks. The other six trade between $1\,000$ and $2\,500$ times. A policy that holds a small number of positions for a long time and loses little while doing so is a different kind of object from one that trades constantly, and the selection criteria of Section~\ref{Subsection:ExperimentalProcedure} were built to let such a model win despite its lower return.

Drawdowns are large elsewhere. Five of the seven pairs reach a maximum drawdown above $40\%$, which is more than most traders would accept on a single instrument. This is the strongest argument for the portfolio construction of Section~\ref{Subsection:Portfolio}, where those drawdowns do not coincide.
