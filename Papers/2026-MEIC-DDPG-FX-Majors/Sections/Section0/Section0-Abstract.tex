\begin{abstract}

Most published work on machine learning for currency trading reports what a strategy earned rather than what it would have kept. This dissertation asks whether a reinforcement learning agent can trade the seven major currency pairs profitably once it is charged what a real account charges, and whether the answer holds when one design is applied to every pair without adjustment. Answering it required building the environment first: a trading framework in which a single strategy implementation runs offline, inside the broker platform's own backtester and on a live account, with an execution engine that steps on individual quotes rather than bar closes, reproduces the commercial platform it was built against byte for byte, and charges the spread recorded at the moment of each trade together with the commission of a swap-free raw-spread retail account, drawing on a database of 1.66 billion quotes. Within that environment one Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) agent was trained per pair, observing thirty-eight dimensionless market features and emitting a single continuous action that sets both the direction and the size of a position, which the discrete action spaces used in almost all prior currency work cannot express. Over eleven years all seven agents are profitable, returning between 15.7\% and 226.6\% net of costs from five different starting balances, and since five of the seven pairs lost value over that period most of this return was earned against falling markets. Six pairs show positive alpha, and on three of them the market exposure, measured as beta against the pair's own movement, is close to zero, while combining the seven into an equal-weight portfolio cuts the maximum drawdown from 34.5\% to 15.9\%. Two findings generalise: the agents win barely more often than a coin, so the edge lies in sizing rather than prediction, and in every pair the highest-returning candidate failed a permutation test, meaning that selecting on return alone yields market exposure dressed as skill.
\keywords{Forex Trading, Deep Reinforcement Learning, Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient, Transaction Costs, Portfolio Construction}

\end{abstract}
