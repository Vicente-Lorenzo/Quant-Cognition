\subsubsection{Forex Market Implementations}

Three of the eighteen articles trade currencies, and all three restrict the agent to a discrete choice between buying, holding and selling.

Carapuço et al. \cite{carapuco_reinforcement_2018} implemented a Deep Q-Network over a fully connected network on raw tick data from 2010 to 2017. The agent was allowed to act only once a fixed number of ticks had passed, which produced a decision roughly every two hours. It outperformed its benchmark by a wide margin, and it is the only study reviewed here that works from tick data rather than from bars. Huang \cite{huang_financial_2018} used a Deep Recurrent Q-Network, which places a long short-term memory network beneath the Q-function, on 15-minute bars from 2012 to 2017. The model outperformed its benchmark with explicit and implicit trading costs charged, which puts it among the few results in the set that survive a cost model. Rundo \cite{rundo_deep_2019} applied the same class of model to one-minute bars from 2004 to 2018 and also reported outperformance, but with an accuracy high enough to suggest look-ahead bias and with a large maximum drawdown. Read together, the two results suggest that adding recurrence does not by itself make very short timeframes tractable.

\subsubsection{Stock Market Implementations}

Equity markets account for most of the work reviewed. Among the critic-only studies, Sim and Kirk \cite{sim_generalized_2023} trained a Deep Q-Network on daily bars from 2006 to 2022 and tested it on assets it had not seen, reporting that the policy transferred. Théate and Ernst \cite{theate_application_2021} used the same algorithm on daily bars from 2012 to 2019 and reported consistent results in calm and volatile periods alike, obtaining exploration from an inverted duplicate of the environment rather than from a noise process. Li et al. \cite{li_deep_2019} compared a Deep Q-Network over a denoising autoencoder against Asynchronous Advantage Actor Critic over a long short-term memory network, on minute bars of stocks and futures from 2010 to 2020; both beat the benchmark, and the asynchronous actor-critic converged better, which the authors attribute to its parallel training.

The actor-critic studies are closer to the design used here. Xiong et al. \cite{xiong_practical_2018} applied DDPG to daily bars from 2009 to 2018 and outperformed the benchmark. Yousefi \cite{yousefi_deep_2022} compared DDPG against Advantage Actor Critic on daily bars from 2009 to 2021, finding DDPG the more stable of the two and crediting its replay buffer and its noise process. Gran et al. \cite{gran_deep_2019} ran DDPG on monthly bars from 2005 to 2019 and kept its advantage once explicit and implicit costs were charged. Azhikodan et al. \cite{saini_stock_2019} added sentiment drawn from news to a state space that already held price data and technical indicators, and reported that the addition improved results, although the evaluation covers roughly five months and the exact period is not stated. Majidi et al. \cite{majidi_algorithmic_2022} used the Twin Delayed variant of DDPG on daily bars from 2010 to 2021 across stocks and cryptocurrencies, with a continuous action in \([-1, 1]\); it beat the cryptocurrency benchmark but not the equity one.

Sagiraju and Mogalla \cite{sagiraju_application_2021} deserve separate mention. They compared four algorithms on daily bars from 1992 to 2020 and, while all four beat the benchmark, Proximal Policy Optimisation and the Deep Q-Network proved the more consistent, with DDPG and Advantage Actor Critic falling below expectation. It is the one study in the set that reports DDPG performing worse than its alternatives, which is a useful counterweight to the studies above.

Two further studies combine algorithms rather than choosing between them. Yang et al. \cite{yang_deep_2020} ran DDPG, Advantage Actor Critic and Proximal Policy Optimisation together on daily bars from 2009 to 2020, switching between them on a turbulence measure, and the ensemble beat the benchmark with explicit costs charged. Kong and So \cite{kong_empirical_2023} combined the same three algorithms on daily bars from 2016 to 2020 and obtained unstable returns and underperformance. The two results together indicate that combining agents is not by itself a source of robustness. Wu et al. \cite{wu_adaptive_2020} sit between the groups, comparing a gated recurrent Q-network against a gated recurrent policy-gradient agent on daily bars from 2008 to 2018, with the policy-gradient variant performing better.

\subsubsection{Other Market Implementations}

Three articles trade instruments outside equities and currencies. Chen and Gao \cite{chen_application_2019} traded exchange-traded funds on daily bars from 2000 to 2018, comparing a Deep Q-Network over a fully connected network against the recurrent variant, with the recurrent model performing better. Zhang et al. \cite{zhang_deep_2020} applied Advantage Actor Critic over a long short-term memory network to futures on daily bars from 2005 to 2019, keeping its advantage after costs. Jia et al. \cite{jia_lstm-ddpg_2021} also traded exchange-traded funds, on daily bars from 2002 to 2020, using DDPG over a long short-term memory network. They tested two variants, one holding a fixed position size and one sizing the position continuously, and the variable-size version performed better with explicit and implicit costs charged. It is the closest study in the set to the design used here, being the only one that uses a continuous action to size a position and charges for trading at the same time.

\subsubsection{Comparative Findings}

Reading the two tables across their columns rather than down their rows shows where the field has settled.

The state space is normally price data with technical indicators derived from it. Studies reporting strong results almost always include indicators rather than price alone \cite{carapuco_reinforcement_2018, huang_financial_2018, yang_deep_2020}. That is consistent with the argument in Section~\ref{Subsection:ReinforcementLearning} that a raw price series is not Markovian, and that indicators fold enough of the recent history into the state for the property to hold approximately.

The action space is discrete in 15 of the 18 studies, usually the three choices of buy, hold and sell \cite{rundo_deep_2019, saini_stock_2019, xiong_practical_2018}. Three studies use a continuous action \cite{majidi_algorithmic_2022, jia_lstm-ddpg_2021, zhang_deep_2020}, and only one of those uses it to size a position rather than to express a direction \cite{jia_lstm-ddpg_2021}. A discrete action set is easier to learn, but it moves position sizing outside the policy, where it has to be set by a rule the agent cannot adapt. The reward is the total return in 14 of the 18 studies. Three use the Sharpe ratio \cite{kong_empirical_2023, sagiraju_application_2021, jia_lstm-ddpg_2021} and one uses the log return \cite{majidi_algorithmic_2022}. A risk-adjusted reward is more expensive to compute during training, since it needs a window of past returns rather than the most recent one, and the studies that use it do not report a consistent advantage from doing so.

Against the bias-variance trade-off the common techniques are feature engineering, normalisation, regularisation and adjustment of the network itself, with denoising \cite{li_deep_2019} and normalisation combined with regularisation \cite{theate_application_2021} as representative examples. For exploration, the \(\varepsilon\)-greedy policy is standard where the action set is discrete and additive Gaussian noise where it is continuous \cite{wu_adaptive_2020, majidi_algorithmic_2022}.

Trading costs are recorded inconsistently. Five of the 18 studies state that their headline result survives explicit and implicit costs \cite{huang_financial_2018, gran_deep_2019, yang_deep_2020, jia_lstm-ddpg_2021, zhang_deep_2020}, and the remainder report returns without recording what was deducted, which leaves the reader to guess whether the figure is gross or net. Training is normally a train-validation-test split, sometimes with cross-validation. One study trains online, continuing to learn as new data arrives \cite{huang_financial_2018}, which is the closest any of them comes to the conditions of live deployment.

The data is long but coarse. Twelve of the 18 studies cover ten years or more, with a median span of eleven years, so a long history is the norm rather than the exception. Resolution is where they are alike in the other direction: 17 of the 18 work on bars, 13 of those on daily bars, and only one uses quote-level data \cite{carapuco_reinforcement_2018}. The distinction matters for cost accounting, since a bar reports four prices and conceals the order in which the price visited them.
